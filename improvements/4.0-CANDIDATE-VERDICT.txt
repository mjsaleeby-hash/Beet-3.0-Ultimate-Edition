VERDICT IN ONE LINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.0 is measurably LEANER and MORE STABLE than 3.0 on the resource axis (fewer
handles, less committed memory, stable working set) with NO correctness or safety
regression. SPEED is not proven either way from field data and would need a
controlled benchmark. The marquee FAT re-copy bug proved to be a NON-ISSUE on this
hardware. Net: ship 4.0; it is at least as good as 3.0 and demonstrably tidier with
system resources.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FAT/exFAT HEADLINE — resolved, and NOT the way the incident doc predicted
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The verification system was originally built to prove the Wave 1.1 FAT re-copy fix.
It turns out there is nothing to prove ON THIS HARDWARE:

  - The BenchmarkHarness `fat` scenario (2026-07-01, real FAT32 USB, 2000-file
    corpus) was run on 3.0-baseline WITH THE FIX NOT APPLIED and re-copied 0 of 2000
    files on the no-change re-run.
  - Root cause: Windows 11 rounds FAT32 last-write-time UP to the next 2-second
    boundary, so the destination ends up 0-2s AHEAD of the source. The
    `source > dest` predicate is never true, so unchanged files are correctly
    skipped. This is the OPPOSITE of what incident-rootcause-incremental-recopy.txt
    assumed (it assumed FAT truncates DOWN).
  - The cohort report's baseline FAT row (median 0, p95 13, n=21) is that single
    benchmark, not organic field backups. The candidate row is "_pending_" and would
    read ~0 as well — a zero delta.

CONCLUSION: the Wave 1.1 `IsDestinationStale` 2-second tolerance still SHIPS as cheap
correctness-hardening (it matches robocopy /FFT, costs nothing on NTFS, and protects
OTHER media that DO round down — SD cards, some NAS/USB bridges). It is proven by
unit tests T1-T5 (in the green 200/200 suite), NOT by an on-hardware benchmark that
structurally cannot show a delta here. Re-running the FAT double-run is therefore
unnecessary and was dropped from the verdict's critical path.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAVEATS BAKED INTO THE NUMBERS (so a future reader isn't misled)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The candidate "1 failed run / 17" is a PHANTOM. On 2026-07-17 the scheduled
   "Users" job SUCCEEDED but ran twice (foreground in-app scheduler racing the
   Windows Task Scheduler --run-job); one process's Running placeholder was stranded
   and later relabelled "Failed - Interrupted". Root cause + proposed fix in
   notes/bugs.md. Subtract it: real failures were 0 on both sides.

2. The candidate "1 crash incident" is the benign shutdown-teardown DllNotFoundException
   (mixed-mode VSS-interop uninitializer during AppDomain unload, after all work
   completed). FIXED 2026-07-24 (commit 164d720, deployed) — foreground exit now
   hard-terminates and skips the CRT teardown. So the candidate's one crash is
   already resolved for any build after 07-24.

3. Field samples are small on the candidate (10-17 per metric). This is why every
   speed row reads "low confidence". The resource/handle/stability wins have adequate
   samples and are the load-bearing part of the verdict.
